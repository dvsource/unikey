# Founder Playbook

_Last updated: 2026-11-02_

How we work, and the guardrails against our known failure modes.

## Known patterns (be honest)
- Viraj tends to abandon projects **once the interesting part is done** (algorithm, UI), **when a competitor appears**, or **when a problem hits**.
- Both of us are shy and afraid of failing in public.

Skepticism is an asset: it's why we did a reality check, researched competitors and set exit criteria before writing code. The risk is skepticism plus fear turning into **quietly stopping**. So stopping has to be a deliberate decision, never drift ([DR-0016](../decisions/0016-stop-only-by-decision.md)).

## Guardrails
| Failure mode | Rule |
|---|---|
| "Interesting part is done" | **Gate building behind customer milestones.** E.g. no scheduler v2 work until 3 teachers are confirmed for the pilot. Build less than you want; a crude v1 that 100 students use beats a beautiful engine nobody sees. |
| Measuring the wrong thing | A visible **scoreboard of users, not commits**: students active today, teachers onboarded, 300s paid. |
| Competitor appears | A competitor changes the plan **only if it hits a revisit trigger.** Open the decision log instead of losing heart. |
| Hitting a wall (expect it around weeks 4–8) | A bad week isn't a verdict; only the pre-written exit criteria are. |
| Stopping | If you stop, write a decision record saying why. "I ran an experiment and learned X," not "I gave up again." |
| Shyness | Use scripts and text (WhatsApp, forms) instead of cold calls. The first circle is close friends. Split roles by comfort: one leads tech and content, the other leads teacher relationships. Then stretch slightly. |
| Fear of failure | The pilot costs some evenings and very little money. The money-back guarantee means nobody loses. Selling here is helping. |

### Commitment device
Tell the 3–4 teacher friends a **pilot start date** and **collect the 300 LKR before we're "ready."** Once real people are waiting, the boring and scary parts become obligations, not choices.

## Talking about the idea ([DR-0017](../decisions/0017-friend-as-advisor.md))
- "Don't jinx it" is real only for telling *for validation* (Gollwitzer, 2009). Telling *for critique and accountability* helps.
- Bring the plan, not the idea. Ask "tell me why this fails." End with a commitment ("I'll show you pilot numbers by March").
- Don't broadcast (social media, a wide circle) until the pilot is running. Then we're showing progress, not announcing intentions.

## Weekly habit
- Post content 3–5×/week.
- Talk to 5 users or parents.
- Run one experiment (a pricing page, a message, a feature).
- Track 5 numbers: signups, activation rate, D7 retention, paid conversion, referral rate.

## Reading list
Read one, apply it the same week. Make Anki cards of the key frameworks.

**Minimum viable list (~15 h, enough for the pilot):** The Mom Test → $100M Offers → Obviously Awesome.

| # | Book | Apply to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Mom Test** (Rob Fitzpatrick) | The next 10 teacher and parent conversations |
| 2 | **$100M Offers** (Alex Hormozi) | Pilot offer, paid tiers, annual plans |
| 3 | **Obviously Awesome** (April Dunford) | One-line pitch, store description, positioning vs AP.LK / BrainUs |
| 4 | **Traction** (Weinberg & Mares) | Bullseye channel testing: teachers vs social vs WhatsApp vs parents |
| 5 | **$100M Leads** (Alex Hormozi) | Teacher affiliate program, content plan |

Later, when the problem shows up:
- **Sales:** SPIN Selling (Rackham), Never Split the Difference (Voss), Influence (Cialdini; use it ethically, the users are teenagers)
- **Messaging:** This Is Marketing (Godin), Building a StoryBrand (Miller)
- **Startup method:** Running Lean (Maurya), The Minimalist Entrepreneur (Lavingia), The Cold Start Problem (Chen)
- **Product / learning science:** Hooked (Eyal), Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel), Why Don't Students Like School? (Willingham)
- **Skip for now:** Zero to One, The Hard Thing About Hard Things, Blitzscaling
