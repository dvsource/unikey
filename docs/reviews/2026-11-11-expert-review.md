## 1. Executive summary

**Verdict: the idea is sound, the documents are unusually good, and the project is on course to miss its own pilot. Continue, but change what you work on tomorrow.**

The product thesis holds up: skill-based spaced practice with auto-graded variants and misconception-tagged distractors is the right design for A/L Physics MCQs, and almost nobody in the local market is doing it. The sample content is better than most commercial MCQ banks I'd expect to see. The decision log and the pre-written exit criteria are a real asset.

The problem is where the last nine days went. Since 2026-11-02 the project has produced 47 decision records, 14 living docs, two design canvases, a tested prototype app (118 unit tests, 19 end-to-end flows), a tested throwaway dashboard (86 end-to-end tests), three skill graphs and 241 sample items. In the same nine days it has produced:

- **0** teacher-reviewed items (every item is `"status": "draft"`, no `review-results.json` exists in any pack)
- **0** written notes from a teacher, parent, student or the advisor friend ([meetings/](../meetings/README.md) holds only the README, and all three planned conversations are unticked)
- **0** confirmed pilot teachers, classes or dates told to anyone ([E01 checklist](../experiments/E01-founding-100-pilot.md#pre-pilot-checklist))
- **0** recruited paid reviewers (the [DR-0035](../decisions/0035-paid-teacher-reviewers-pilot.md) trigger fires on 2026-11-30)
- **0** real phones the app has run on (ENV-04 in the [QA report](../../app/qa/test-report.md), "not run")
- **0** lines of the real backend, scheduler, sync, payments or study loop

Your own [founder playbook](../living/founder-playbook.md#guardrails) says "gate building behind customer milestones" and "a scoreboard of users, not commits". Both rules are being broken, and the pattern is the one you named yourself: the interesting part (engine design, UI, tooling) is absorbing the time, and the scary part (asking teachers, collecting 300 rupees, hearing your wife say an item is wrong) has not started.

There are 65 days to mid-January. The work still planned for those days, for one person who is also job-hunting, is roughly 4–5 months of work (section 4). Something will give. It is better to choose what gives now than to find out on January 10.

### The 5 most important changes

1. **Start the human work this week, before any more building.** Your wife reviews the Mechanics sample in the reviewer app (the README says about 3 hours). You confirm the pilot teachers, their classes, batch years and current units, and tell them the date. This costs under 10 hours total and replaces half the guesses in the docs with facts.
2. **Cut the pilot to what a 5-minute-a-day student can actually touch.** A retained pilot student will make roughly 300–500 attempts in 8 weeks (section 2, problem 1). That is enough to work on 40–60 skills, not 175. Scope content to about 70 high-exam-weight skills in the units the pilot classes are actually studying, and review only those. This turns an impossible review load into a feasible one.
3. **Replace the pilot engine, backend, simulator and dashboard with the simplest thing that produces the pilot's answers.** One scheduling rule set on the phone, events uploaded to one table, the teacher digest generated by a script and sent by hand. No API server, no job worker, no simulator through the real backend, no `/admin` page, no sim engines, no widget, no term-test mode. Keep the mid-January date by starting small (your wife's own class first), then add the other teachers in February.
4. **Settle the government-teacher question for your wife before she sells anything to her own students.** DR-0040 removed the affiliate share to avoid a conflict of interest, but the bigger conflict is still there: a government teacher's household receiving 100% of the revenue from a paid app she recommends to students she grades. Verify with a lawyer or the relevant circulars before January.
5. **Design the pilot to produce proof, and give students a trigger.** As written, the mock-score test can't fail (scores rise in any class over 8 weeks) and can't convince anyone. Use the uncovered units as a built-in control. And a habit product for teenagers with no reminder of any kind is betting D30 on willpower: allow one student-set daily reminder.

If you do only the first one this week, the project is in better shape than it is today.

## 2. Domain expert: A/L education, learning science, tuition culture

**Verdict: the learning design is strong and the content is good. The dosage claim, the outcome test and the fit with how A/L students actually spend their time are the weak points.**

### Keep

- **Skills, not cards, with new variants each time** ([DR-0004](../decisions/0004-schedule-skills-not-cards.md)). This is the correct answer to "Anki doesn't work for physics". Repeating a fixed numerical MCQ trains answer recall.
- **L3 "which method?" items.** Interleaved method-selection practice is one of the best-supported ideas in the learning literature for problem subjects, and nobody local does it. The pools read well, for example `phy.mech.p04-03` (moments about the hinge vs the string).
- **Distractors computed from named mistakes.** Templates like `phy.mech.t02` (ball passing a height twice) carry a specific message per wrong answer ("That's the speed at that height, not at launch"). This is the product's real differentiator, more than the scheduler.
- **Syllabus-first skill graphs, with past papers mapped afterwards** ([DR-0022](../decisions/0022-typed-skill-graph.md)), and soft gating instead of locks.
- **Holding back predicted marks** until calibrated. Correct, and it should stay held back for a long time.
- **Item quality.** I checked the arithmetic and physics of about 50 cards across the three units by hand and found no wrong answer key. Numbers are calculator-free and A/L-like. That is a high bar to have cleared with AI drafting.

### Problems, ranked

**1. The dose is too small for the promise, and the data plan assumes six times more practice than the product asks for.**
- The product asks for 10 questions a day ([DR-0019](../decisions/0019-teen-retention-design.md)) and the exit criteria want 4 days a week ([DR-0009](../decisions/0009-pilot-exit-criteria.md)). A student who meets both does 40–60 items a week: 300–500 attempts and about 3–5 hours of practice across the whole 8-week pilot.
- [DR-0008](../decisions/0008-pilot-close-circle.md) assumes "~100 students × ~30 items/day × 60 days ≈ 150–180k attempts". With 5-minute days, 4 days a week and a 30% D30, a realistic total is 15,000–25,000 attempts (guess, but the arithmetic is simple). That is one-seventh of the plan. Item calibration, misconception statistics and the "data moat" all shrink with it.
- The three pilot units hold 175 skills (83 + 46 + 46, from the three `graph.json` files). At 300–500 attempts a student gets 2–3 attempts per skill if spread evenly. A mastery estimate needs at least 3. So a pilot student can meaningfully work on 40–60 skills. The rest of the graph is decoration for the pilot.
- The promise "5 minutes a day… so nothing you learn in class is forgotten" ([strategy](../living/strategy.md#positioning)) is not true at this dose once a student has more than a unit or two behind them. The whole syllabus will be 400+ skills ([DR-0027](../decisions/0027-content-plan-stop-point.md)). Implicit review helps, but 10 items a day cannot keep 400 skills alive. Five minutes is the floor that keeps the habit; it is not the dose that moves marks. Say so, or a parent will rightly feel misled.

**2. The outcome test can't fail and can't prove anything.**
- The exit criterion is "mock MCQ score delta, week 0 vs week 8… clear positive gain", with a control group only "if possible" ([DR-0009](../decisions/0009-pilot-exit-criteria.md), [E01](../experiments/E01-founding-100-pilot.md)).
- Any class improves over 8 weeks of teaching and tuition. So the gain will be positive, the guarantee ([DR-0021](../decisions/0021-usage-tied-guarantee.md)) will almost never pay out, and the result will convince no skeptical teacher. "Clear" has no number.
- The dose makes it worse: 3–5 hours of app practice against 60+ hours of school and tuition physics in the same 8 weeks. A whole-paper mock will not detect it.
- Fix (cheap): a **within-student control**. Build the week-0 and week-8 mocks with half the questions on skills the app covers and half on skills from the same units that it does not cover. Compare each student's gain on covered vs uncovered skills, and heavy users vs light users. No control class needed, and the result is publishable.

**3. No item has been checked by a teacher, and the weakest parts are the ones only she can judge.**
- Every item in all three packs is `draft`. The graphs say `"status": "draft", "reviewer": null`.
- **Generic mistake tags dominate two of the three units.** I counted the wrong options on L2/L3 items whose tag is one of `ignored-condition`, `wrong-law`, `inverted`, `wrong-quantity`, `wrong-power`, `assumed-symmetry`, `recall-gap`: Mechanics 25%, Current Electricity 52%, Thermal Physics 75%. DR-0027 fix 3 ("ban catch-all tags, check each tag matches the mistake") was done for Mechanics only. A mistake notebook that tells a student "you ignored a condition" 29 times teaches nothing, and the teacher dashboard's "shared misconception" card will be noise for those units.
- Some tags are plainly arbitrary: in `phy.therm.c034` (relative humidity of two rooms) the option 130% is tagged `sign` and 70% is tagged `inverted`; in `phy.mech.c025` 90 m s⁻¹ is `inverted`.
- **A card that depends on another card:** `phy.therm.c043` begins "The same copper bar, now unlagged". Under spaced repetition it will be shown alone, weeks after c042. The automated checks didn't catch this class of error.
- **Sinhala (flags for your wife, verify):** `phy.mech.c034` uses ජවය for power; I believe the A/L syllabus term is ක්ෂමතාව. "අචල ප්‍රතිරෝධකය" (fixed resistor) and "අචල ස්කන්ධය" (fixed mass) read as "immobile"; "මුද්‍රා තැබූ වායුව" (sealed gas) reads as "stamped". The Thermal [review doc](../../content/samples/thermal-physics-20/review.md) itself lists 10 glossary conflicts, including glossary typos. This is translation-ese that a teacher will spot in seconds, and it's exactly the "one wrong term destroys teacher trust" risk in [strategy](../living/strategy.md#biggest-risks).

**4. Coverage is one-third of the graph, and the notes don't exist.**
- Items exist for 56 of 175 skills (18/83, 19/46, 19/46). Only those 56 have a Sinhala name; the app shows the other 119 in English with "No questions yet" ([app/README](../../app/README.md)).
- The [content spec](../living/content-authoring.md#9-coverage-targets-per-skill) requires one micro-note per skill. I found no note content anywhere in `content/`; items only carry a `note_ref` id. Learn mode ([DR-0005](../decisions/0005-learn-mode-notes-and-sims.md)) has no content at all yet.

**5. The plan underweights how A/L students actually live.** (Mostly guess; this is what the device-and-usage survey, open topic T10, should have answered already.)
- An A/L student typically has school plus tuition for three subjects, often with a separate theory class, revision class and paper class per subject. Free time is scarce and phones are often restricted by parents in the A/L years and banned at school.
- The physics authority in a student's life is usually the **tuition teacher**, not the school teacher. Grade 13 school attendance drops in the last months. The pilot teachers are "mostly government school" ([teacher dashboard §1](../living/teacher-dashboard.md)). A school teacher's "teaching now" may not be what the student is doing in tuition that week.
- Which batch are the pilot students? The docs never say. A 2027-batch (Grade 13) student in January is finishing the syllabus and starting revision; a 2028-batch (Grade 12) student is early in it. Mechanics, Thermal and Current Electricity fit those two groups very differently. This decides whether "the queue follows the class" is even possible in the pilot.

**6. The engine is far more complex than the pilot can exercise.**
- [DR-0022](../decisions/0022-typed-skill-graph.md) specifies two-dimensional mastery, FSRS for cards, Elo for skills, implicit review weights, adaptive placement from Knowledge Space Theory, inferred mastery, and a two-level exam weight. Every one is defensible. None can show its value in 300–500 attempts per student, and review debt (the thing implicit review exists to prevent) cannot build up in 8 weeks at 10 items a day.
- The readiness number is an honest idea with a display risk: "34 → 37" will be read as marks out of 100, whatever the label says.

### Recommendations

- State the dose honestly: "5 minutes keeps your streak; 15 minutes moves your marks." Keep 5 minutes as the minimum day.
- Adopt the within-student mock design (proposed DR-0052). Write the two mocks in November; they need teacher review too.
- Your wife's first 3 hours go to the Mechanics sample; her next hour goes to the 10 glossary conflicts. Do not generate more Sinhala until the glossary is fixed, or every new item inherits the errors.
- Re-run the tag pass on Electricity and Thermal (DR-0027 fix 3). Add an automated check for "the same", "now", "as before" in card stems.
- For the pilot, run one simple scheduler (proposed DR-0051) and keep the full DR-0022 design for after real data exists.
- Ask each pilot teacher which batch, which unit now, which units by March, and whether students have their own phones. Five WhatsApp messages.

### Decisions I think are mistaken

- **DR-0020 (no push)**: see section 3; proposed DR-0049.
- **DR-0021 (guarantee tied to mock score)**: the score condition is unfalsifiable and the practice-days condition reads as a catch. Proposed DR-0057: "Practise 20 days. If you don't think it helped, we refund the 300." Worst case costs 30,000 LKR and buys goodwill and honest signal.
- **DR-0009** stays as accepted, but its terms are undefined (what is D30? study days per week among whom?). Proposed DR-0052 adds definitions without touching thresholds.

## 3. Product manager

**Verdict: problem and solution fit is plausible but unproven; the MVP is not an MVP; the paywall and trigger decisions work against the one metric that gates everything.**

### Keep

- First win before sign-in, optional name, pre-filled units ([DR-0026](../decisions/0026-onboarding-flow.md)).
- No review debt, "Done for today" as a real ending, no-guilt comebacks ([DR-0019](../decisions/0019-teen-retention-design.md)).
- Student-controlled parent sharing ([DR-0018](../decisions/0018-student-is-primary-customer.md)). Right for 17–19-year-olds.
- Actions-not-charts for teachers ([DR-0037](../decisions/0037-teacher-dashboard-principles.md)), with "Useful / Not right?" on every card.
- The reviewer mode in the prototype. It is the best-spent engineering so far, because it puts content in front of the scarce reviewer the way a student sees it.
- The "Your path today" trail and the skill detail screen (new on 2026-11-11). Showing *why* each stop is in today's queue ("Refresh", "New", "Recommended first") is a real differentiator and makes the graph visible without a scary map.

### Problems, ranked

**1. The pilot "MVP" is the v1 product.**
The [roadmap v1 list](../living/roadmap.md#feature-versions) for the pilot: SRS cards and questions, L3, L4 templates, timed MCQ, readiness score, weak-topic report, mistake notebook, micro-notes, 3 sim engines, teacher dashboard, parent report, 5-minute day, streak freeze, no-guilt catch-up, home-screen widget, opt-in WhatsApp reminder, term-test mode. Add the onboarding A1–A9, anonymous-to-account merge, phone OTP through a local SMS gateway, the paywall, activation codes, class join and sharing levels, the simulator and `/admin`. Open topics B2 and T4 ("MVP scope freeze") are still marked **Open** in [open-topics](../living/open-topics.md) on 2026-11-11.

The pilot has to answer four questions: do students come back, does practice move covered-skill scores, will parents pay, will teachers look at the digest. Most of that list answers none of them.

**2. No trigger at all.**
- [DR-0020](../decisions/0020-no-push-notifications.md) bans push because you find it annoying. The replacements are a home-screen widget (unbuilt; adoption of widgets is usually a small minority of users, guess), an opt-in WhatsApp reminder that is off by default and sent by hand in the pilot, and friend features that are not in the pilot ([DR-0026](../decisions/0026-onboarding-flow.md) item 5). [DR-0038](../decisions/0038-quiet-students-by-name-no-nudges.md) also stops the teacher nudging through the app.
- So on day 3, nothing in the student's world mentions UniKey. For a daily-habit product whose gate is D30 ≥ 30%, this is the single riskiest product decision in the log. Onboarding screen A3 already asks the student for their routine time; it then does nothing with the answer.
- A reminder the student sets for themselves, once a day, at the time they chose, with an easy off switch, is not nagging. It is the product keeping a promise the student made.

**3. The paywall comes before the product's value is visible.**
- [DR-0030](../decisions/0030-paywall-after-first-session.md) puts the paywall after one 10-question session calibrated to 80% correct. At that point UniKey looks like any free quiz app. What makes it different (a skill coming back just before it fades, a mistake pattern, the trail changing) needs at least a week.
- After the pilot the payment path is: student taps "Ask a parent", parent reads a WhatsApp, makes a bank transfer or pays a link, someone sends a code. That takes hours to days, with the student locked out the whole time. Most will not come back (guess, but a safe one).
- The record's own consequence line admits it: "One session may be too little to show value."
- This doesn't affect the pilot (Founding 100 prepay). It matters for April. Proposed DR-0050: 7 days of full access with no card, then the paywall, with "Ask a parent" available from day 3. Still no permanent free plan, so [DR-0029](../decisions/0029-no-free-plan.md) stands.

**4. Progress screens will look empty.**
With content on 56 of 175 skills, the Progress rings and chips will be mostly "No questions yet", and 119 skills show English names inside a Sinhala product. For a student this reads as "unfinished app". Either hide skills without content or scope each ring to skills that have content. Also, the prototype marks a skill "mastered" when its few sample items were each last answered right ([app/README](../../app/README.md)); don't demo that state to teachers as real mastery.

**5. The docs disagree with each other, which will mislead whoever builds next (including AI sessions that read them).**
- [go-to-market](../living/go-to-market.md) (last updated 11-03) still says "Close with a free trial for one batch", ends posts with "Practise 20 more like this free", and its first teacher message promises a paper builder, teaching materials and simulations. None exist, and [DR-0029](../decisions/0029-no-free-plan.md) removed anything free.
- [pricing](../living/pricing.md) says teachers get "a fixed 100 LKR/month affiliate share"; [DR-0040](../decisions/0040-affiliate-for-all-teachers.md) removed it for the pilot and for government teachers.
- [strategy](../living/strategy.md#positioning) leads with "5 minutes" and two lines later the Bio message says "15 minutes a day".
- [product-spec](../living/product-spec.md#first-7-days) day 2 is "Add a friend… first duel"; DR-0026 removed friend invites from week 1 and the pilot has no social features.
- The product spec's [MVP table](../living/product-spec.md#mvp-cut-physics-pilot) lists 3 sim engines and micro-notes as "build now".

**6. Measurement isn't ready.** The analytics event list is "missing" (T5). D30 has no definition. "Activation" is undefined. There is no single number you look at each morning.

### Recommendations

- **Freeze a pilot scope this week** (proposed DR-0048). In: bundled content packs for the chosen skills, a 10-item session, one simple scheduler, streak with freeze, mistake list, the trail, the skill detail screen, activation code as the login, event upload, a weekly mock set, one student-set reminder. Out, until after the pilot: sim engines, micro-notes as a separate Learn mode (the per-item explanation is the note for now), widget, term-test mode, friend features, phone OTP, anonymous merge, adaptive placement (use "which units have you covered?" plus the teacher's "teaching now"), parent web report (send a text), live teacher dashboard.
- **Concierge the teacher and parent side.** A script reads the event table every Sunday and writes each teacher's digest text; you paste it into WhatsApp. This tests whether teachers care (the DR-0009 criterion) without building S1–S9, auth and row-level security in December.
- **Pick the daily number:** students who practised today ÷ students who paid. And the weekly number: students with ≥ 3 practice days this week.
- **Define the events now** (10 is enough: install, activation, session start, item answered, session done, reminder set, reminder tapped, mock done, parent message sent, digest link opened).
- **Do one doc clean-up pass** on go-to-market, pricing, strategy and product-spec so they match the accepted DRs. An hour's work.

### Decisions I think are mistaken

- **DR-0020** → proposed **DR-0049** (student-set reminder).
- **DR-0030** → proposed **DR-0050** (7-day trial before the paywall, post-pilot).
- **DR-0005 and DR-0006** (3 sim engines and micro-notes in the pilot) → folded into proposed **DR-0048** (defer both).
- **DR-0042** (live dashboard at pilot start) → folded into proposed **DR-0048** (manual digest first; live dashboard when teachers ask for more).

## 5. Sales manager

**Verdict: there is no sales motion yet, only a plan for one. The channel you have (government school teachers) is warm but structurally weak; the channel that can reach 1,000 payers (tuition teachers) has no offer designed for it.**

### Keep

### Problems, ranked

**1. The government-teacher conflict was only half removed.**
DR-0040 says a government teacher "earning from a paid app they recommend to the students they also grade is the conflict MoE circulars target". That describes your wife exactly, and not for a 100-rupee share: her household keeps the full price. Removing the affiliate share for her changes nothing about that. Separately, public servants in Sri Lanka generally need permission for outside business activity (verify; I am not a lawyer and don't know her terms of service). Legal question L2 is still open, and the pilot plan has her recruiting her own students for a paid product in eight weeks. One complaint from one parent to one principal is the scenario DR-0040 itself fears. This needs an answer before the first 300 is collected, not after.

Possible shapes, to discuss with someone qualified: the business is yours alone and she has no financial or public role in it; her own school students are offered the pilot free or by a different teacher; she pilots only with students she doesn't grade; or she gets written clearance.

**2. The arithmetic of the channel.**
- A government school physics class is perhaps 20–40 students (guess). At a generous 20% paid conversion that is 4–8 payers per teacher. A thousand payers needs 125–250 actively recommending school teachers. The 500-teacher channel at a 10–20% opt-in ([A12](../experiments/assumptions.md)) gives 50–100 teachers, of whom some fraction stays active.
- A mid-size tuition teacher has 100–500 students in one subject. At 10% conversion, 20 such teachers reach 1,000. [Go-to-market](../living/go-to-market.md) puts them at "+3–6 months" and big players at "+6–12 months", after the school-teacher channel.
- The channel order is chosen for warmth, and that is right for the pilot. It is the wrong order for revenue. At least one tuition teacher should be in the conversation by February.

**3. The offer to tuition teachers is the wrong currency.**
100 LKR per paying student per month ([DR-0034](../decisions/0034-teacher-offer-free-tool-plus-affiliate.md)) is 5,000 LKR a month for a teacher who converts 50 students. A tuition teacher with 300 students earns many times that from one class (guess). The money won't move them. What might: results they can advertise, students who don't drift to a rival, and something that makes their class feel modern. The offer should be built around those: their name on the class edition, their weekly quiz running in the app, a class result report they can show parents.

**4. The teacher pitch promises things that don't exist.**
The hook in [go-to-market](../living/go-to-market.md#selling-points-in-order) is the paper builder, then teaching materials and simulations. None are in the pilot, the paper builder has "its own conversation" pending (TD1), and the plan needs a paper-builder demo by pilot weeks 4–6 for the 500-channel pitch, on top of everything in section 4. The doc itself says "only promise what exists at rollout". Either the paper builder gets cut from the first message, or it gets tested by hand first (idea 9.6).

**5. "No free plan" plus "class-code students pay the same" makes the teacher's ask hard.**
The teacher must tell students "pay 900 a month for an app covering 3 of 11 units, after one free session". [DR-0029](../decisions/0029-no-free-plan.md) lists "teachers refuse to recommend an app their students must pay for before trying" as a revisit trigger. I expect it to fire in the first conversation with a non-friend teacher. The 7-day trial (proposed DR-0050) fixes most of it.

**6. Price against coverage.**
900 a month is defensible against tuition for a full-syllabus product. For 3 units out of 11 it is about 27% of the subject at full price. Parents will do that sum. Consider pricing by coverage until the syllabus is complete (for example 500 a month, rising as units ship, with founders locked), or sell a term pass.

**7. Monthly manual payment will not scale and will churn.**
A parent making a bank transfer and sending a slip every month, and you sending a code back, works for 100 and is your full-time job at 500. Every monthly renewal is also a decision point where the family can stop. Default to longer plans (term or until-exam, which [DR-0032](../decisions/0032-until-exam-price-by-months-left.md) already prices well) and treat monthly as the exception.

**8. Google Play's payment rules may forbid the planned flow.**
The plan is a paid-only app with payment outside the app and an "Ask a parent" button that sends a payment link ([ux-design](../living/ux-design.md), flow E). Play's policy generally requires Play Billing for digital content consumed in the app, and restricts in-app steering to outside payment (verify the current policy for Sri Lanka; this is legal question L3, still open). A closed test will probably pass unnoticed; a public listing may be rejected or removed. If Play Billing is required, the 15% fee comes off every sale and the unit economics in [pricing](../living/pricing.md#revenue-math) move to the bad end of their range. Settle L3 before designing the real paywall.

### Recommendations

- Resolve problem 1 first. It is the only item in this review that could end the project by itself.
- Run one tuition-teacher experiment during the pilot (proposed DR-0054, idea 9.1): a bundle where the teacher adds the app to the class fee and pays you a wholesale price per student.
- Rewrite the first teacher message to promise only: "your class's weak spots, on WhatsApp, every Sunday" plus whatever else truly exists that week.
- Write the pilot teacher pitch as a script your wife can deliver in five minutes, and the parent message in Sinhala. Both are TODOs in go-to-market since 11-03.
- For the pilot, log every "no" with its reason. The objections of 30 parents who declined are worth more than the 70 who paid.

### Decisions I think are mistaken

- **DR-0034** (100 LKR affiliate as the tuition-teacher offer) → proposed **DR-0054** (test a class bundle with a wholesale price; keep the affiliate share as the fallback).
- **DR-0030** → proposed **DR-0050**, as in section 3.
- **DR-0014** later stages are still "Proposed"; they can't be accepted until L3 is answered.

## 9. Ideas for success

Bold but doable. Each is marked with what it costs to test.

**9.1 The class edition: sell to the tuition teacher, not through them.**
A tuition teacher adds the app to the class fee (say +300 to +400 per student per month) and pays you a wholesale price per enrolled student. Every student in the class has it, so the class league, the teacher's "teaching now" and the digest all work at full strength. There is no parent payment conversation, no activation code per student, no Play Billing question (it is a business invoice), and no affiliate ledger. Three teachers with 350 students each is your 1,000. The price per student is lower than 900, but conversion is close to 100% of the class instead of 10%, and the teacher does the collecting. *Test:* one conversation with one tuition teacher your wife knows, in February, with wave A data in hand.

**9.2 The teacher's weekly quiz, inside the app.**
Tuition culture already has the "paper class" and the weekly test. Let the teacher pick 10 items from the bank (or the app picks them from "teaching now") as this week's class quiz. Students do it in the app after class, it is auto-marked, and the teacher gets the result and the most-missed question for next lesson. This gives the student a class-linked reason to open the app every week without push and without UniKey nudging anyone. It also quietly answers the question DR-0020 raised ("we can't force every student to enrol"): nobody is forced, but the class has a reason. *Test:* by hand in wave A. Your wife announces a quiz, the app shows a fixed set, you send her the results.

**9.3 Past-paper X-ray: a product for the 2027 batch that needs no new items.**
A student does a real past paper on paper, as every A/L student already does, then taps their 50 answers into the app. Because the past-paper questions are already mapped to skills (116 Mechanics, 58 Thermal and the Electricity map exist in the `question-map.json` files; the content spec mentions 500 mapped MCQs), the app returns "you lost 14 marks; 9 of them come from these 4 skills" and a plan. No copyrighted text is shown; the student holds the paper. This needs a question-to-skill map for all 11 units, which is far cheaper to produce and review than items. It gives the Grade 13 crowd a reason to pay in April–July even where practice content is thin, and it feeds the best data you could have: real exam-question error rates per skill. The prototype's design canvas already lists "Question X-ray" and "Past-paper lights" as next picks; this is the same idea turned into the lead feature for that cohort. *Test:* do it by hand for 10 of your wife's students with one paper and a spreadsheet.

**9.4 Publish the mistakes.**
After the pilot, publish "The 20 mistakes A/L Physics students make most in Mechanics", with real percentages from your data, in Sinhala. Teachers will share it because it's useful in class and makes them look informed; nobody else can produce it; and it shows what the product does better than any feature list. Give each pilot teacher their own class's version first. This is the data moat made visible. *Cost:* one query and a designed PDF.

**9.5 Reviewer names on the content.**
"Reviewed by [name], [school]" on each unit, with the teacher's permission. Teachers get public credit, which matters in that community; parents get a trust signal; and each reviewer becomes an advocate in the 500-channel. It also makes the paid-reviewer role something a teacher wants on their record, not just piecework. *Cost:* a line of UI and a consent sentence in the reviewer agreement.

**9.6 Concierge paper builder.**
Before designing a paper builder, make papers by hand. Offer each pilot teacher one term-test MCQ paper a month, generated from the bank by script and checked by your wife, as a PDF with a marking scheme. If they use it in class, you have validated the hook in [A11](../experiments/assumptions.md) and you know what a paper needs to contain. If they don't, you've saved the build. *Cost:* a few hours a month.

**9.7 Sponsored seats.**
Every class gets a few free seats that the teacher gives to students who can't pay, quietly. It costs almost nothing at the margin, it gives a government teacher a reason to recommend the app that is plainly not commercial, and it is the right thing to do in a country where tuition cost is a real barrier. It may also be part of the answer to section 5 problem 1. *Test:* 3 seats per class in the pilot.

**9.8 English medium is nearly free.**
Every item is authored in English first and then translated (the `medium: "en"` source and `text.en` fields in every pack). English-medium A/L students are a smaller group but concentrated in urban schools with higher ability to pay (guess), and they need no translation review, only the physics review you're already doing. Turning it on after the pilot roughly costs a language switch. Sinhala stays the pilot medium ([DR-0031](../decisions/0031-pilot-scope-3-units-sinhala.md)). *Cost:* small, after the pilot.

**9.9 Repeaters.**
94,004 of the 340,525 candidates in 2025 were private candidates ([strategy](../living/strategy.md#market-size)), many of them repeating. They have no school teacher, they are adults (no consent step), they are highly motivated, they often pay for themselves, and they have the whole syllabus behind them, so "what have I forgotten?" is exactly their question. Nothing in the docs targets them. The X-ray (9.3) is the natural entry point for them. *Test:* ask the pilot teachers how many repeaters attend their classes.

**9.10 Term pass instead of monthly.**
Sell by school term (about 3–4 months) as the default unit, priced a little under three monthlies, renewing at the term boundary when the term test has just shown the student where they stand. Fewer payment events for you to process by hand, fewer chances to churn, and the renewal moment comes with fresh evidence. It sits naturally beside the until-exam plan. *Test:* offer it to Founding 100 students at the end of the pilot and see which plan they choose.
