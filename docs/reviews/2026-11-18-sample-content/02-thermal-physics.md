# Thermal Physics sample review

**Verdict.** The calculations are consistent with the intended A/L models. The kinetic-theory notation and two Sinhala translations change what the student is being asked to learn. I would correct these before using the affected items to judge understanding.

| Existing item | Finding | Suggested wording or correction |
|---|---|---|
| **phy.therm.c024, c026** | The printed key ⅓ρc̄² puts the bar over c alone. The square of mean speed and the mean of squared speeds differ. The c024 explanation names the intended quantity, while the formula visually encodes the other one. Grade 12 guide 4.4, printed p. 54, connects pressure with molecular speeds. The cited 2019 Q15 explicitly gives the rms speed. | Print **p = ρ⟨c²⟩/3 = ρvᵣₘₛ²/3**. Explain “එක් එක් අණුවේ වේගය වර්ග කර, එම අගයන්ගේ මධ්‍යන්‍යය ගන්න.” The c026 value stands. 3 × 120,000 / 600² = 1.0 kg m⁻³. |
| **phy.therm.c021–c023, b02** | The helium–oxygen comparison is valid for mean **translational** kinetic energy per molecule. “මධ්‍යන්‍ය අණුක චාලක ශක්තිය” leaves rotation in scope for oxygen. The Sinhala guide gives the translational quantity with the 3RT/(2Nₐ) expression on printed p. 54. | Use “අණුවක මධ්‍යන්‍ය උත්තාරණ චාලක ශක්තිය” in the stem, key and statement bank. Preserve the 1 ∶ 1 key in c023. |
| **phy.therm.c003, b01** | “Too small” became “ඉතා කුඩා” (“very small”). The issue is the direction of measurement error. A hot tape can read only slightly below the true length. The same translation affects both true and false statements on calorimetry errors. | In c003 use “සැබෑ දිගට වඩා අඩු ය” and “සැබෑ දිගට වඩා වැඩි ය” for the opposing choices. In b01 use “ගණනය කළ අගය සැබෑ අගයට වඩා අඩු වේ / වැඩි වේ.” Retain each statement’s original truth value. |
| **phy.therm.t02, c014, b01** | The quantity in J kg⁻¹ is specific latent heat. Calling it simply “ගුප්ත තාපය” hides the distinction between L and the heat mL required by the sample. The syllabus explicitly names specific latent heat of fusion in 4.6. | Use “අයිස්වල විලයනයේ විශිෂ්ට ගුප්ත තාපය” for L. Use “අයිස් දිය කිරීමට අවශ්‍ය තාප ප්‍රමාණය” for mL. |
| **phy.therm.c008, t03** | “හානි වූ තාපය” suggests heat lost to the surroundings. The intended heat leaves the hot metal and enters the water. The distinction matters because the question explicitly neglects environmental heat loss. | “ලෝහයෙන් පිට වූ තාප ප්‍රමාණය ජලය ලබාගත් තාප ප්‍රමාණයට සමාන වේ.” For the ideal calculation, also neglect the vessel’s heat capacity. |
| **phy.therm.c011** | The final state is defensible, but 12.6 < 35.7 alone proves only that the ice cannot all melt. It leaves out the check that some ice can melt. | Give the energy to warm the ice to 0 °C as 2.1 kJ in the stem. Then explain that 12.6 − 2.1 = 10.5 kJ remains for melting, below the 33.6 kJ needed to melt it all. The final mixture is ice and water at 0 °C. |
| **phy.therm.c039, c040** | Both explanations presume expansion that does external work. In insulated free expansion of an ideal gas, Q = W = ΔU = 0. The gas can expand at constant temperature without heat entering. This makes the unconditional true/false wording especially troublesome for c040. | Specify expansion against a moving piston with positive external pressure. For c040, “පරිපූර්ණ වායුවක් පිස්ටනයක් තල්ලු කරමින් නියත උෂ්ණත්වයේ දී ප්‍රසාරණය වේ.” Then Q = W > 0. |
| **phy.therm.c041, c043** | The ¾ drop across the lower-conductivity rod assumes steady one-dimensional flow with insulated sides. The unlagged-bar graph assumes a cooler environment. Merely giving θ₁ > θ₂ cannot determine the curvature. | Give c041 the same insulation assumption as t09 and state steady conditions. In c043 make the cold end equal to room temperature and the hot end warmer. In the Sinhala explanation say “අනුක්‍රමණයේ විශාලත්වය අඩු වේ.” The negative slope approaches zero. |
| **phy.therm.c009, c015–c017** | “අචල” can suggest stationary. The questions hold heater power or gas mass constant. | Use “නියත ජවයකින් ක්‍රියා කරන තාපකයක්” and “නියත ස්කන්ධයක් ඇති පරිපූර්ණ වායුවක්”. For c016, naming the axes directly also reads more naturally than “T ට එරෙහිව”. |

**Examples to retain.** The dew-point term “තුෂාර අංකය” and relative-humidity calculation in c031 agree with guide 4.7, printed p. 58. The available vapour pressure is 2.3 kPa, so 100 × 2.3/4.2 rounds to 55%. The first-law example c035 explicitly defines work done by the gas, which makes the +300 J answer in c036 straightforward.
